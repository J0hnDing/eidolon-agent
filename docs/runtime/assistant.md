# Primary Assistant

Assistant has two main jobs: **understand the user** and **help the user**. Understanding grows through conversation about the user's experiences, feelings, motivations, interests and circumstances. Help includes practical action and emotional company. A personal question can serve both purposes without needing a productivity outcome. Important events normally lead to a planned personal follow-up through the existing agenda; the user's response guides conversation, support and any practical improvement. Ordinary conversations and background activations use the same resumable thread protocol, tools, agenda, and natural response schema.

## Agenda and curiosity

Shared Assistant instructions prioritize understanding the user's present day when that context would improve help or timing. Recent conversation, calendar and responsibilities form a partial picture; a sparse calendar does not establish availability. The Assistant can ask about plans, current activity or how the day is going, using existing answers and fitting the question to the moment without turning it into a productivity check. Timely situational questions take precedence over unrelated curiosity from old records. A scheduled common question can be rescheduled for a more relevant conversation; important and temporal questions retain their urgency. This is standing behavior across conversations and activations, rather than another morning-planning step.

One `assistant_agenda` table stores tasks and questions. Each item has a title, notes, kind, open/waiting/done/dismissed status, optional next activation time, optional calendar recurrence for tasks, and optimistic revision. Notes hold personal context, outcome, evidence, uncertainty, dependencies and next steps as needed. Questions can preserve personal follow-ups, companionship or something worth understanding better; their conversational wording is chosen when asking. A question need not be persisted if the current exchange resolves it.

Agenda items are Assistant responsibilities, linked to authoritative user todos, Atlas records and events rather than copied into another task system. The UI supports creation, editing, resolving, dismissing, reopening and deletion. Conflicting edits fail; completed/dismissed items clear their activation time. Repeated task creates with equal kind, title, notes, due time and recurrence reuse an active item. Active questions also deduplicate titles across case, whitespace and trailing punctuation, regardless of notes or timing; renaming into an existing active title fails. Semantic overlap is model judgment: before adding/reopening a question the Assistant must inspect the paginated whole question list, including answered questions, and reuse/refine an existing question or its answer rather than create a paraphrase. assistant.agenda.list supports kind filtering.

Planning saves concrete future responsibilities; their agenda activations execute the work. Morning planning deliberately selects at least **two concrete work items, two personal interactions and one additional useful item** for today. All five must be distinct, ordinary nonrecurring agenda responsibilities. Existing items can count when reviewed and scheduled; no minimum number of newly created items applies. Work means an actual useful result the Assistant takes responsibility for, rather than planning, housekeeping or asking the user to do it. Interactions may be questions, personal follow-ups, casual conversation or companionship; no particular topic, wording or question quota is prescribed. The other item is an additional useful responsibility of the model's choosing. The morning feed and recurring routines do not fill these slots. The model chooses content around the user's practical concerns, relationships, wellbeing, interests and goals, preserves history and avoids duplicate or unanswered questions.

Every Morning planning attempt must freshly read todos and goals regardless of detected changes. It reads every `notion.todo.list` page and reviews unfinished/unarchived overdue, due-soon, unscheduled and blocked work. It reads `atlas.goal.list(limit=100)` without pursuit/horizon filters, reviewing native goal details and relationships; a list reaching the limit requires a complete paginated native-data Goal search instead of assuming completeness. Changed-record summaries, cached source metadata, the feed and prior daily reviews do not replace these reads. Date-only targets, real deadlines, stale unchecked tasks and completed events are reconciled from current evidence. Paused/abandoned goals are reviewed as context rather than restarted automatically.

The morning procedure turns overdue review into concrete next steps before unrelated new work: verify completion, do an authorized part, prepare material for approval, ask for missing information, or deliberately defer with a reason and next review time. Related todos can be handled together. Unclear completion status leads to a focused question; flexible deadlines and no-late-penalty policies affect urgency without establishing that work is done. Small ready steps can be handled during review and substantial work scheduled promptly. Preparatory research should remove a real obstacle rather than replace available action or a needed question. The daily selection minimums do not replace attention to the backlog. Responsibilities remain linked to authoritative todos through existing agenda notes.

Unanswered questions are reconsidered in relation to blocked work. An approaching deadline, changed circumstances, continued blockage or elapsed time relative to urgency can justify refining or reopening the existing question and asking or scheduling a purposeful follow-up. Earlier waiting notes are a previous decision to review, not a permanent prohibition; explicit refusals, pauses and no-contact requests remain authoritative. Duplicate questions and repeated messages within the same exchange are avoided, and the answer resumes the linked work. These are morning agenda instructions using existing tools, not a new reminder scheduler or per-todo database requirement. Permission and academic boundaries still apply. The user-facing morning message stays selective and has no mandatory backlog report or fixed structure.

Authenticated MCP reads record small per-turn `assistant_review_json.planning_sources` receipts after bounded output validation: source status/counts and pagination coverage, never source bodies or todo/goal notes. Full todo cursor traversal and an unfiltered complete goal read are checked by the morning finish validator and crash reconciliation; partial/filtered/stale reads cannot satisfy the requirement. Each retry reads sources again. Actual source failures or trusted live capability/permission unavailability remain explicitly unavailable, not reviewed/empty; they allow a transparent blocker path alongside the existing feed/plan checks. The prompt requires disclosure of the source gap and any needed input/access. Backend checks verify read coverage and availability; judgment about relevance, honest disclosure and choosing/performing the right work remains the model's responsibility. Fresh morning turn context includes `planning_review` to make remaining source coverage visible.

After scheduling the selected items, the authenticated private `assistant.plan.save` tool accepts `work`, `interaction` and `other` arrays of agenda IDs, with minimum sizes 2/2/1 and at most 30 distinct IDs in total. Work IDs must identify tasks; interaction and other IDs can identify tasks or questions. Pending selections need an activation time on today's date in the current Assistant timezone. Recurring, deleted, dismissed, future-day and unscheduled items cannot satisfy the plan; waiting blocked responsibilities and unanswered questions cannot fill slots merely by scheduling another review. Already executed work or initiated interactions can count with actual evidence today. The tool saves a receipt on its Assistant turn, preserving selection after agenda activation/completion clears `due_at`; there is no duplicate task store or new scheduler.

Runtime and fresh context expose `daily_plan`: required, planned and completed counts, selected item status/timing, deficits, planning-turn ID and whether the plan and its follow-through are satisfied. Actual successful one-off agenda activation plus done state supports work completion. Question initiation uses durable question-message receipts and does not require an answer. Casual-interaction tasks require their own successful activation and actual Assistant output, using canonical archived content when necessary. A task activation may satisfy at most one personal interaction; planned casual interactions activate separately. The morning briefing and unrelated replies do not count as these interactions. The backend verifies observable planning/execution/delivery facts; relevance, distinct meaning, actual result quality and truthful classification remain model judgment.

Later activations execute the plan, preserve result evidence and revise blockers instead of silently dropping responsibilities. Selected tasks left open without another wake time retry review after 15 minutes; a casual interaction marked done without a message is reopened for review. Unmodified selected tasks interrupted or failed retain uncertainty notes and a 15-minute review time. Explicit user cancellation, deliberate waiting/deferral, deletion and dismissal are respected. Approval and no-contact/quiet-hour constraints take precedence over filling a minimum; the Assistant must disclose actual constraints and choose useful authorized alternatives.

At least one meaningful user-visible Assistant interaction is required per local calendar day while proactive mode is enabled. A relevant question, personal follow-up or useful result counts; a user reply is not required. Silent turns, plans, saved summaries and internal refresh messages do not count. `daily_interaction` in runtime/current context reports the local day, timezone, required minimum, actual output count, satisfied state and last interaction time. Counts include ordinary final responses and live Assistant messages in Assistant chats, using canonical archive content after SQLite erasure. Act/Observer output and unanswered user messages do not count. Existing unanswered questions do not prohibit a different relevant interaction. Explicit user requests about no contact and quiet hours still apply.

One-time tasks and questions use the same agenda activation time (`due_at`). Recurring tasks have backend-managed `due_at`; questions cannot recur. Questions have a `question_category`: `common` (the default for existing and new items), `important`, or `temporal`. Important questions need prompt attention because delay matters or blocks authorized work; notes explain why. Temporal questions matter now or at a particular moment, such as 30 minutes before an interview. Open important/temporal questions without a time become due immediately; explicit future times are preserved. Common questions without a time remain available for the Assistant to plan during reviews or conversation. There is no separate question scheduler, daily slot assignment, expiry field or automatic expiry. Category is meaningful only for question items.

The model paces questions naturally around recent exchanges, engagement, commitments, quiet hours and answering effort. An unanswered question can coexist with a different timely conversation or personal follow-up. There is no strict two-question limit, fixed asking window or minimum spacing. Important and temporal questions follow their urgency or intended moment. Future temporal questions are scheduled at their intended time and asked when due, without waiting for a related conversation. If Eidolon was offline or execution delayed, the model checks present circumstances before asking. The backend must be running and an existing running turn can delay activation; agenda times are not hard real-time delivery guarantees.

Due questions arrive in the same activation agenda as tasks. The Assistant asks through `assistant.question.ask`, resolves already answered questions, dismisses obsolete ones, or explicitly reschedules common questions when attention is scarce. The tool checks current revision and open state, respects a future `due_at`, and can ask an unscheduled open question during a running turn. It posts natural wording through the existing chat/Telegram path and changes the item to waiting. The Questions tab displays categories, scheduled times and a descriptive daily count without a quota. Memory also exposes the same shared Agenda and Questions controls, plus paginated daily summaries and retained conversation archives; these views do not create additional memory stores. Runtime `question_asking` exposes `asked_today`, `last_asked_at` and timezone as attention context.

The durable receipt is `act_live_messages.agenda_question_id`. Receipt and waiting state commit together before Telegram I/O. They survive agenda deletion and restart; repeated calls for a question still waiting return its existing receipt across local-day boundaries, including after an uncertain provider outcome. A failed remote send can still leave the question visible in web Chat. An evaluated question that was neither asked nor revised stays open and retries evaluation after 15 minutes. Asked questions are not automatically repeated; they wait until answered, dismissed or deliberately reopened. Silence is not an answer. Wording, classification, attention management, semantic overlap and routing proactive questions through the receipt tool are model responsibilities. Ordinary clarifications needed for a user-requested task remain part of that task.

When an answer arrives, the Assistant must connect it to the pending question, save it as stated knowledge with evidence to a suitable Atlas record, or use a fact outside Atlas if no category fits naturally, then mark the question done with the answer and saved record/memory reference. It revises related questions and plans from the answer. Saving and deciding the appropriate knowledge category use existing model/tool workflows. If storage fails, question notes retain the answer and pending save rather than asking the user to repeat it.

## Activation and continuity

One backend timer checks every 30 seconds. It schedules ordinary due agenda items and recurring tasks through one activation path while proactive Assistant is enabled and the permanent conversation is idle. Pending user work takes precedence. Assistant agenda scheduling is independent of generated service schedules.

With Muse access enabled, a new live Muse-initiated message becomes an ordinary
due agenda conversation through WhatsApp maintenance. It follows the same activation
and user-work priority rules. Duplicate/history/stream-edit events cannot start
another conversation. Its ledger carries discussion state and flexible guidance of
roughly 30 total messages across both sides through subsequent reply continuations;
the Assistant follows ideas and feedback freely and closes naturally. See
[WhatsApp](../integrations/whatsapp.md) for account, permission and cache boundaries.

Task `recurrence` is `null` or an object with `frequency` (`daily`, `weekly`, `monthly`), `time` (`HH:MM`) and an IANA `timezone`. Weekly schedules include `weekdays` (Monday 0 through Sunday 6); monthly schedules include `day_of_month` (1–31). Months without the selected day are skipped. Calendar times stay local across daylight-saving changes: an ambiguous time runs once in its first fold, and a nonexistent time shifts forward by the DST gap. New or changed schedules establish a fresh activation boundary. `open` enables the recurring task, `waiting` pauses it, and `done` or `dismissed` stops it. Completing an occurrence does not complete the recurring task. Users can edit or delete any recurring item, including the initial Morning planning and Evening reflection tasks.

Each selected recurring task runs alone in its Assistant turn. A durable occurrence identifies the agenda item, schedule version and scheduled instant, with `queued`, `succeeded`, `failed` or `skipped` status, linked turn, attempt count, retry time and completion time. A database claim commits before dispatch to prevent duplicate scheduling. The task instructions run with the Assistant's normal tools, permissions and approval requirements; there is no built-in task category, execution flag, or separate reflection runtime.

After downtime, the scheduler recovers only the most recently missed occurrence of each active recurring task. It does not queue a backlog for earlier calendar dates. A queued occurrence that becomes outdated before model execution is rejected and superseded by the latest due occurrence. Failed or interrupted occurrences retry after 30 seconds while they remain the latest occurrence; an older failed occurrence becomes skipped when a newer scheduled occurrence is due. Retry context includes the prior turn so the Assistant must inspect evidence and verify previous external effects before continuing. Turn success marks the occurrence succeeded; task-specific instructions and completion checks remain model responsibilities. Manual Run now uses the same path and creates an immediate occurrence when no due occurrence needs recovery, without consuming the next scheduled run.

Morning planning and Evening reflection are ordinary editable recurring agenda tasks, initially daily at 09:00 and 22:00 America/Toronto respectively. Morning planning's notes require fresh todos and goals, then `assistant.changes.list`, the wider context and agenda, concrete help, specific input requests and saved responsibilities for later activation. Evening reflection's notes instruct it to summarize important actions actually completed and decisions made on the occurrence's local calendar date, using `assistant.memory.history.read(day, timezone)` for retained Assistant, Act and Observer conversations. It writes 1–1200 characters through `assistant.memory.summary.write(day, summary)` to `memory/daily-summaries/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, including a brief explicit note for an uneventful day, then returns an empty final response. Its notes ask it to avoid unrelated work and messages. Those are editable instructions under normal Assistant authority, not backend tool restrictions; the backend does not turn the final answer into a summary automatically.

Morning planning reviews the broad context and whole question list, explicitly reads current Atlas States through `atlas.states.get` without an `at` timestamp, and reads relevant configuration/history when needed. It considers individual goal, interest and relationship estimates, recency and confidence alongside the user's wishes and circumstances; States inform judgment rather than dictate the plan. Stale progress can justify asking whether something still matters or offering to find a free block, but does not establish disinterest. It creates or meaningfully revises concrete agenda responsibilities rather than advancing substantial work in that turn, saves the 2/2/1 selection, then sends the morning feed described below. A third ordinary editable task, Daily check-in, initially runs at 21:00 in the Assistant timezone as a fallback for the daily interaction requirement. It checks today's actual contact and finishes silently if the requirement is already satisfied; otherwise it produces a useful grounded question or brief natural message. This legacy daily-contact fallback is independent of the two planned personal interactions and cannot replace them. Timely event follow-ups should be scheduled independently instead of waiting for the fallback.

Daily check-in uses `completion_requirement: interaction_today`: at least one actual Assistant output must exist on the current local day. Morning planning uses `completion_requirement: morning_plan`: today's valid saved 2/2/1 plan and an actual morning briefing from this occurrence are required. The normal finish hook and crash reconciliation enforce both requirements. An unmet requirement fails the occurrence with an inspectable reason and retries after 15 minutes; transport/execution failures retain the ordinary retry rules. Morning retries may reuse today's valid saved responsibilities and an already delivered briefing from the same occurrence's attempts. Unrelated prior messages cannot satisfy the briefing check. Planning success means the plan and briefing exist, not that the day's work is already complete.

An idempotent upgrade changes only the exact untouched old Morning planning notes and attaches `morning_plan`, including installations that already completed the earlier planning migration, preserving customized instructions, titles, status and schedules. It seeds Daily check-in once (reusing a same-named recurring task if present). Deleting/editing these tasks afterward is respected. Existing customized Morning planning notes require deliberate revision to adopt the new procedure; this installation was updated explicitly for the user's requested change. Its dated historical review was removed from reusable instructions and preserved in a local diagnostic snapshot. Daily decisions/results belong in relevant agenda/source records or the daily summary instead of accumulating in the recurring procedure. Either completion requirement cannot be removed or changed while its task still recurs; removing recurrence clears it. Daily contact and plan dates use the Assistant's current timezone, independently of a task's scheduled timezone. The backend must be running and tools/contact permissions available for delivery. Offline time does not generate a backlog of old daily messages.

The backend enforces one permanent Assistant chat across web, Telegram and background activations. Opening or creating Assistant returns that same chat, and its archive endpoint directs the user to refresh instead. Legacy chats and their completed history are retained as archived records; queued work is moved to the permanent chat with its original delivery snapshot. Agenda, source snapshots, pending changes and folder memory live independently of the Codex thread. Fresh context includes a compact current agenda, memory locations and three recent daily summaries; it never includes other chats or a reconstructed user/assistant transcript. Tool pagination accesses additional agenda items and full notes.

### Prompt ownership and context

The Assistant aims for the judgment of an attentive human personal assistant: useful work and conversation fitted to the person and the moment. Before speaking it chooses the contribution of the message, rather than turning its work record into a report. Shared instructions define ordinary first-person language, brief acknowledgments, direct recommendations, selective links, and examples spanning completed work, advice, research, partial failure and personal follow-up. Requested detail still gets fuller treatment. Material uncertainty, incomplete requested work and blockers remain explicit when they affect expectations or decisions; routine permission checks, verification and lists of actions not taken stay in the records. These instructions apply to every conversation, background result and live message.

`agent_instructions/assistant.txt` is the Assistant's Codex base prompt, replacing Codex's built-in coding-agent instructions on both thread start and resume. The same text is not also sent as developer instructions; that slot is explicitly cleared, including when resuming an older thread. It owns the role, initiative, relationship, communication, authority and general agenda behavior. Agenda titles and notes should be concrete and concise, retaining task-specific constraints and material evidence without copying shared policy checklists or whole reviews. Editable recurring notes own task procedures: Morning planning specifies fresh source reads, reconciliation, today's 2/2/1 selection, saving the plan, briefing content and retry requirements; it does not redefine the Assistant's personality or general behavior. Evening reflection similarly owns its review and summary procedure. Known unchanged morning defaults upgrade once to the shorter procedure; customized notes, one-time work, schedules and deleted routines are preserved. Tool descriptions own pagination, review receipts, history reads, summary limits and schedule-editing contracts. Workspace `AGENTS.md` owns the shared file and memory map.

Managed Assistant, Act and Observer processes disable Codex's global memory injection and generation using process and thread configuration overrides. Personal context continues to come from Atlas, the shared Eidolon `memory/` folder and the agenda. These overrides do not change the user's global Codex settings or delete existing memories or conversation history. Restart the backend to load changed prompt/configuration code, then refresh the Assistant to start a clean thread without previously injected Codex context; resuming an older thread does not rewrite its historical messages.

A recurring turn's task prompt is the current agenda notes, or its title when notes are empty. There is no recurring-task wrapper. Execution revalidates the task and reads its latest notes, so note edits made while queued take effect without changing the recurrence. The activation retains the task ID, revision, kind and title, plus occurrence date, timezone, attempt and prior-turn metadata; it does not repeat the task notes. This also removes obsolete wrappers and duplicated notes from legacy queued activations at the model-input boundary without rewriting their stored claims.

The first 30 active agenda entries in prompt context retain IDs/revisions, titles, kinds, status and due times. Recurring definitions include their schedules; their notes are read through `assistant.agenda.list` when needed. Other responsibility notes retain the existing 1500-character bound and explicitly indicate truncation. Administrative timestamps, session/last-turn pointers and occurrence histories stay available in the agenda API but are omitted from prompt context. Change-source context retains pending counts, last successful collection and errors; routine poll timestamps are omitted. Action context includes pending approvals, queued/running work and terminal outcomes awaiting Assistant evaluation, filtered before the 20-item bound. Evaluated historical actions are omitted. Source collection, permissions, approval requirements and review receipts are unchanged.

Session-title guidance is supplied only when a chat needs a title. Titled Assistant, Act and Observer turns retain the same response object but constrain `title` to `null` through the output schema, without a title metadata prefix. The schema is copied per turn so titled turns do not alter title generation for a new chat.

`AssistantTurnDispatcher` owns the serial Assistant worker, separate from Act dispatch. Its Codex App Server process and thread remain live between successful turns. The daily session window runs from **04:00 to the next 04:00 in the configured timezone** (America/Toronto by default). The idle dispatcher creates a fresh Codex thread when the boundary is reached, without requiring a message or invoking the model. `/refresh`, the Assistant settings button, or `POST /agents/assistant/runtime/refresh` requests the same transition as soon as the dispatcher is idle. A running turn finishes before the transition. The permanent chat retains visible history, but prior session transcripts and backend chat messages are not supplied to the fresh thread. Current Atlas, folder memory and agenda remain available. A task can explicitly read its requested day's archive through the private history tool; fresh threads do not automatically replay that archive.

Startup compares the saved thread with the most recent local 04:00 boundary. A missed boundary invalidates the old thread durably before any queued work can resume it; the idle dispatcher then creates one fresh thread. Multiple missed days coalesce into one refresh, not one model turn per missed day. Startup within the same 04:00-to-04:00 window resumes the saved thread; a missing rollout creates a fresh thread with current context only, never transcript replay. Failed or cancelled turns discard the process connection and revoke its credentials before recovery. Successful turns retain session-bound MCP and browser credentials until refresh or shutdown; tool calls still recheck current agent policy. New tools/configuration are discovered at refresh. Runtime status exposes `thread_started_at`, `refresh_pending`, the fixed `refresh_time` of `04:00`, and `next_refresh_at`. Each boundary is constructed in local wall time so daylight-saving changes keep refresh at 04:00. Failed idle refreshes retain a pending thread identity and retry after 30 seconds; ordinary user requests may also attempt recovery.

Before reusing a live process for another turn, the dispatcher checks the exact MCP credential against current database state. If it was revoked or replaced, the backend reconnects and resumes the same daily Codex thread with a fresh session-bound credential. The old credential stays revoked; normal current policy checks still apply. A live process alone does not establish tool access.

Activation metadata is stored separately on the turn. Internal activation prompts and silent successful results are hidden from chat. A useful final answer is delivered through the paired private Assistant chat. Empty background answers do not send a fallback notification. Attention and quiet-hour judgments are model rules, not guaranteed suppression by a separate notification engine.

Claims persist before execution. Interrupted/failed one-time agenda evaluation leaves visible waiting items for the next review. Recurring occurrences retain their durable claim and retry state described above; retries require checking prior effects. Interrupted outcome evaluation likewise leaves a responsibility with evidence to investigate. Queued user messages remain eligible; a live reply to chat.send_message is consumed within that same running turn. A delivery destination is saved with queued work; startup reconciles pending terminal results. Atomic delivery claims prevent duplicate sends from overlapping workers. A send interrupted after its claim has an uncertain provider outcome and is marked failed instead of replayed.

### Daily feed briefing

Morning planning (initially 09:00 America/Toronto) now sends the feed after saving the plan, through the normal final-response chat/Telegram delivery. Its editable notes own the procedure. The former separate 09:05 **Daily feed** agenda item was paused on this installation to prevent a second briefing; arbitrary same-named customized tasks are not automatically paused or recreated by source migrations.

The morning message has no required topics, sections, ordering or length. Calendar, backlog and plan review prepare the Assistant to help; they are not a required user-facing summary. Depending on current circumstances it might ask whether a listed activity is still the plan and what else the user is doing, share a useful discovery, bring a timely decision or continue a relevant conversation. Weather is an optional example when an outing or commute makes it useful. Examples in the editable notes are possibilities rather than a checklist. A useful question can itself be the morning message, delivered once through the ordinary reply or the tracked-question tool. The morning message does not itself satisfy either planned personal interaction.

Questions may be scheduled for their own agenda activation or asked during planning. A nonempty tracked question actually delivered in the morning occurrence satisfies its morning-message requirement, including when its content has moved to the canonical chat archive. The morning validator also recognizes delivery from an earlier attempt of the same occurrence, avoiding a second send merely because planning was retried. It still requires current source coverage and a valid saved plan. This does not change how separate planned interactions are credited, and an unanswered tracked question retains its ordinary waiting state and duplicate-delivery receipt.

The configured standalone Notion page remains an optional source through `notion.daily_feed.read`; check the report's own local date, not just its edit timestamp, when using it. Verify time-sensitive details chosen for the message. Weather retrieval is optional, and source-availability reporting belongs in the message only when a gap materially affects the help or decision at hand. Required fresh todo/goal review and honest handling of unavailable sources still apply. The deterministic Notion producer remains independently enabled or paused through its service controls. This change does not run or enable it, change its fixed report format, or require Notion for morning planning. Substantial research stays in later agenda activations so the morning message can be sent promptly.

## Initial Atlas understanding

The Assistant performs a finite reading pass over the connected Atlas before treating its initial understanding as established. The backend automatically activates this work when the primary conversation is idle and proactive Assistant is enabled. User conversation keeps priority. The pass resumes across turns and restarts; it is independent of ordinary compact baseline changes and is not satisfied by merely receiving record metadata.

The pass reads current schemas and custom fields, every live record and its relationships, the goal graph, Knowledge nodes with their connections, States configuration/current estimates and observation history, personality and completeness information, and record attachments. It follows source pagination and retrieves full record/node/link details. Large documents are delivered in explicit bounded slices; resumes are read through their text content, and images are sent as actual MCP image content for visual analysis rather than base64 text. Trash and archived record revision histories are outside the initial pass. An attachment that cannot be read leaves the pass blocked rather than silently counted as understood.

The backend owns a persistent queue of source references, pagination/slice positions, completed batch count and retry state in `assistant_state.initialization_json`. It does not persist a second copy of Atlas bodies or image bytes. The Assistant-only `assistant.initialization.read` tool supplies the next bounded batch. A batch advances only when it was read, the model called `assistant.initialization.acknowledge` for the delivered batch, and that turn succeeded. Failure, cancellation, missing acknowledgement and interrupted work retain the batch. Temporary blocks retry after 15 minutes. Changed sliced documents restart from their beginning; already completed work is preserved. A different connected Atlas starts a new pass.

The pass reads current data as it progresses, rather than a historical snapshot at its original start time. Current States use Atlas's current-time read, including when resuming an older queued task that stored a timestamp. This allows a pass to continue after States were initialized or reconfigured later. Explicit historical States reads outside initialization retain their requested timestamp.

The model uses each batch to build a coherent understanding of the user's circumstances, goals, motivations, interests, relationships and unresolved tensions. Atlas remains authoritative; initialization does not require a duplicate knowledge or continuity store. It checks the complete question list before adding meaningful unresolved questions and refines overlapping questions rather than creating paraphrases. There is no question-creation quota: the Assistant schedules common questions considerately and important/temporal questions at the moment they matter. Answers continue to update Atlas first, or facts outside Atlas when no category fits naturally.

A final synthesis batch revisits the accumulated understanding and outstanding questions before initialization becomes complete. Backend receipts establish full delivery and acknowledged review, while interpretation and semantic question quality remain model responsibilities. The Assistant page shows pending, reading, blocked/retrying or complete status, completed batch count and currently remaining steps; discovered detail pages may increase that remaining count.

## Backend change detection and review tracking

When `assistant.changes.list` requests a review, fixed backend collectors read supported sources through existing integration authorization and the Assistant's current permissions. They compare complete snapshots by stable item ID, revision and content hash. Source state holds only compact metadata, hashes and retrieval references; it does not duplicate Atlas personal fields, email bodies, notes or conversation transcripts. The model retrieves authoritative detail when needed and decides relevance, implications and action.

Collection covers Atlas records and links, configured Notion todos, primary Google Calendar events from the previous month's start through three months ahead, Gmail/Outlook conversation summaries since the previous month's start, and unexpired facts in `memory/facts-outside-atlas/`. Email detection covers summary changes, not body edits invisible in those summaries. Drive, Atlas Knowledge, States and attachments have no recurring change collector here; Atlas content is covered by the initial reading pass, and model research can still use existing tools afterward. Collection is bounded to 100 pages and 10,000 items per source. These are observed snapshots, not a provider event log: intermediate edits between polls may be coalesced, and external APIs without snapshot guarantees may change during a scan.

The first successful scan creates baseline items, which are existing context rather than newly occurring events. Later scans create added, updated or removed_from_scope changes. A scope/window shift uses entered_scope for newly visible items and never interprets dropped window membership as deletion. Removal from any observed collection is not proof of provider deletion. Connection/account/resource changes create a separate source identity. Incomplete pagination, bounds, permission failures and unavailable sources preserve the previous successful snapshot and pending changes. Sources advance independently, with visible last-check, last-success and error status.

Changes remain pending independently of polling. `assistant.changes.list` supplies the first 30 items and their total, and paginates additional items. Only pages actually supplied to a turn are claimed by it. Successful turn completion records their review in the same transaction as terminal success; failure, cancellation and interruption leave them pending for a later review. New changes arriving during a review cannot be acknowledged by that earlier turn. Successful analysis may remain silent. Review success records a completed model turn, not a guarantee of correct judgment; unresolved work belongs in the agenda. Old pending changes require checking present circumstances before acting.

Collection is prepared once per turn and survives prompt recovery, including empty/failed collections. Ordinary chat does not poll sources automatically; assistant.changes.list can request collection for a conversational review. A turn only receives changes from sources successfully collected under its current authority. Runtime status exposes source availability and pending counts. Occurrence timestamps measure scheduling, source success timestamps measure collection, and individual change receipts measure completed review. There are no model-managed source cursors or change-review acknowledgement tools. Initialization uses its separate explicit batch acknowledgement.

## Understanding and authority

Atlas owns structured personal understanding. The shared workspace `memory/` is the only agent memory store. Atlas editable-content operations travel through the normal integration authorization, schema validation and audit path: record/link create and update, trash/recovery and revision restoration, custom fields, States configuration/activity/manual estimates, and image/resume upload/download/deletion. Atlas validates native invariants and optimistic revisions where supported. Reads remain discoverable through atlas.describe and the existing read tools. Record data/custom values are complete replacements, so read first and preserve untouched values. Irreversible Trash purge, field deletion (including historical values) and attachment deletion require backend per-call approval. Knowledge changes, Personality file import and vault administration are outside this expansion. See [Atlas integration](../integrations/atlas.md) for contracts and native limits.

The shared Assistant instructions require active States maintenance after relevant completed events and feedback. It normally asks about the user's experience, but can update from sufficient direct evidence without another question. It reads current estimates/history and checks for an existing outcome before writing activity or observation entries to individual goal, interest or relationship targets; Atlas derives aggregate States. Evidence distinguishes user reports, observations and inferences, with appropriate confidence. A plan, silence or elapsed time alone is insufficient to invent progress or satisfaction. Meaningful uncertainty is clarified through conversation and the existing agenda, updating or resolving related questions as evidence arrives. For now Assistant ignores `physical_activity` and `mental_stimulation` in active planning and States updates; `physical_energy` and `mental_energy` remain relevant. These are model instructions using existing tools, permissions and agenda, without another scheduler or automatic event detector.

`AgentMemoryService` stores editable JSON facts in `memory/facts-outside-atlas/`, concise reflection notes in `memory/daily-summaries/`, and complete visible messages in `memory/chat-history/{assistant,act,observer}/`. No working-preference or convention taxonomy has been defined. Facts retain stated/observed/inferred provenance and evidence; corrections compare the current value. User edits mark attribution stated. Atlas is preferred whenever an appropriate category exists; avoid copying Atlas records into facts. Hypotheses remain hypotheses and sensitive deductions still require an explicit request.

`memory/chat-history/` is the sole persistent store of completed Assistant, Act and Observer message content. It includes full ordinary user messages, final agent answers and live exchanges, with timestamps and session/turn references. Internal activation prompts, tool traces and reasoning are excluded. Daily summary tool writes live in the separate daily-summaries folder; an empty task response creates no chat message. Chat acceptance, live messages, terminal turns, cancellation, chat archiving and startup synchronize the archive. Once file writes and receipts are durable, terminal `act_turns.user_message`/`assistant_message` and associated `act_live_messages.text` are erased and `history_archived_at` is set. Queued/running content stays in SQLite until completion. IDs, status, activity, errors, transport bindings, approval/review receipts and agenda metadata remain operational database state.

The archive retains at most 30 days and 50 MiB across all three agents, pruning the oldest whole messages without truncating retained text. Retention runs at startup, on chat access/synchronization and every minute independently of proactive Assistant. Durable cursors and archive markers prevent expired or manually deleted files from reappearing. API responses, missing-rollout recovery, pending delivery and completed-action evaluation read retained content from files without writing it back into ORM rows. Pruned messages disappear from Chat; expired pending replies are not sent. A permanent Assistant chat keeps its identity while its old content expires. Browser storage holds managed-chat metadata only, and reading a legacy cache scrubs its transcript copy; Project conversations remain locally persisted.

Migration transfers existing completed database content before erasing it, preserves deliberately discarded files, and retries interrupted cleanup from durable receipts. Failed file writes leave SQLite content available for retry. SQLite secure deletion overwrites cleared cells; startup compacts freed pages outside active agent work. Runtime metadata remains in SQLite, so the transcript retention cap is not a cap on all application data.

Workspace `AGENTS.md` documents this map for all agents, preserving unrelated user guidance. Files are editable/deletable. The agenda remains in its existing tools/database and is not copied into memory. Workspace `knowledge/` holds reference material and `workspace/` holds working artifacts.

Assistant has Act's filesystem grants, command networking, live search, Playwright browser, private authentication and current-conversation live/file delivery tools. It has the same default function permissions. Observer retains its existing read-only defaults. Current explicit bans, integration authorizations and per-call approvals still apply.

The model judges low stakes using explicit autonomy rules: concrete effects, scope, costs, recipients, uncertainty and recoverability. It performs authorized research, preparation and routine work directly. Consequential commitments, submissions, spending, external contact, disclosure and destructive changes require approval unless already authorized. A durable approval request preserves the reviewed instruction; acceptance starts Act through the existing atomic proposal path. Natural conversational decisions quote an actual current user message or recorded live reply in the same conversation, after the action was presented. An activation alone cannot supply consent. Editable legacy function sequences require approval through their current UI review. Pending requests and repeated calls within the originating turn are deduplicated; a later request after a terminal decision may create a new approval. Results wake Assistant for fresh evaluation. Approval does not grant tools otherwise blocked by backend policy.

Notion reports are optional deliverables. An unavailable Notion connection does not fail a review or prevent local progress. The default experience surfaces useful questions, decisions and results; tool activity remains inspectable.

## API and migration

- GET/PUT `/agents/assistant/runtime` for proactive `enabled` and the Assistant/session-refresh `timezone`; POST `/agents/assistant/runtime/refresh`. There is no special daily/reflection run route.
- GET/POST `/agents/assistant/agenda`; PATCH/DELETE `/agents/assistant/agenda/{id}` with expected revision. Create/update accepts nullable `recurrence`; omit `due_at` for recurring tasks because the backend manages it.
- POST `/agents/assistant/agenda/{id}/run` with `expected_revision` runs an active recurring task. GET `/agents/assistant/agenda/{id}/occurrences` returns up to 100 occurrences, newest first.
- Existing session and proposal approval endpoints remain.
- Assistant-only private tools: agenda list/create/update, question ask, initialization read/acknowledge, changes list, memory history read/summary write, remember, request approval and record a user approval decision. Public MCP, Observer and generated skills do not receive them. The old `assistant.context.update` tool and writable runtime summary are removed.

Startup migrates legacy database facts into `memory/facts-outside-atlas/` with IDs/provenance preserved and the old continuity note into `memory/daily-summaries/legacy-continuity.md`, then clears those legacy values only after verified file writes. Receipts make migration idempotent and preserve later user edits/deletions. Historical notes are not fabricated as daily reflections. Existing unrelated workspace memory files are preserved. A one-time schedule migration creates ordinary Morning planning and Evening reflection agenda tasks from the legacy times, timezone and successful-run timestamps; current queued legacy activations are mapped to their occurrences, while outdated queued activations are superseded by the latest due occurrence. A known failed or pending legacy morning claim is not imported as a successful run. A durable migration marker prevents recreation after user edits or deletion. Legacy daily/reflection configuration and timestamps remain inert compatibility columns used only for migration; the global proactive switch is unchanged.

First initialization converts outstanding legacy follow-ups into agenda questions and retires their sender state. Null or malformed legacy follow-up payloads are skipped, including SQLite JSON null values. Completed historical proposals are retained without generating a burst of outcome notices. Existing Assistant policy read-only defaults are migrated to write-capable while explicit bans are preserved. The database upgrade removes the old model-managed checkpoint column and starts fresh backend baselines; agenda and conversation state remain. Other legacy database tables/columns and the internal Codex routing key `assessment` remain for stored-data compatibility; the active assessment workflow and API are removed.

The backend must be running for activations. There is no OS wake mechanism or guarantee of delivery while the computer is asleep. On restart the next timer reconciles due work; failed reviews retain their pending changes, and successful review receipts are reconciled idempotently.

The Assistant Telegram bot uses its paired private chat directly. Topic support, topic creation and topic mappings are not required. Ordinary messages, background results, live questions, approvals and workspace files use that one chat. Act and Observer keep their topic-based transports. Assistant file delivery requires the running turn's current snapshotted Telegram connection/chat; re-pairing cannot redirect it. Replacing an Assistant connection retires pending/uncertain deliveries instead of replaying them to the replacement binding.
